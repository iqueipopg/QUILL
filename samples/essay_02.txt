Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly being integrated into healthcare systems worldwide, promising improved diagnostics, personalized treatment plans, and more efficient resource allocation. However, this technological advancement raises significant ethical concerns that must be carefully examined.

The first ethical dimension concerns patient privacy and data security. AI systems in healthcare require vast amounts of patient data to function effectively. The collection, storage, and processing of sensitive medical information creates vulnerabilities. For example, IBM Watson Health faced criticism when reports emerged about potential data sharing practices that patients had not explicitly consented to. The tension between the need for large datasets to improve AI performance and the individual right to privacy presents a fundamental ethical challenge.

Second, algorithmic bias represents a critical concern. AI systems trained on historically biased datasets may perpetuate or even amplify existing healthcare disparities. A notable example is the algorithm studied by Obermeyer et al. (2019) in Science, which was found to systematically underestimate the health needs of Black patients compared to equally sick White patients. This bias arose because the algorithm used healthcare spending as a proxy for health needs, failing to account for systemic inequalities in healthcare access.

Third, the question of accountability when AI systems make errors is particularly pressing. When an AI-assisted diagnostic tool fails to identify a tumor or recommends an inappropriate treatment, determining responsibility becomes complex. Is the physician who relied on the AI recommendation liable? The hospital that deployed the system? Or the developers who created the algorithm? Current legal frameworks are inadequate to address these questions.

In conclusion, while AI holds tremendous potential to improve healthcare outcomes, the ethical implications of privacy, bias, and accountability require proactive governance. Regulatory frameworks must evolve alongside the technology, and interdisciplinary collaboration between technologists, ethicists, and healthcare professionals is essential to ensure that AI serves all patients equitably.
