QUILL.

Quintet of Intelligent LLMs for Learning.

QUILL decomposes essay feedback into five specialised LLM agents — each operating under a focused prompt, communicating via strict JSON schemas — instead of asking one model to evaluate structure, content and language all at once.

The problem

Single-prompt feedback dissolves under its own ambition.

Ask one large language model to evaluate structure, content and language all at once and the feedback gets thinner, not richer. The model averages its attention across dimensions, hedges, and produces the kind of paragraph that sounds like feedback without committing to a specific change the student can act on.

The result is consistently generic prose — fluent, kind, and forgettable. Concrete errors that a teacher would mark in the margin get absorbed into the discourse, if they appear at all. The rubric goes in, but the response that comes out has no scale left to defend.

Condition A · single-prompt baseline
"You have written a heartfelt and engaging essay about a person who significantly impacted your life, and your story is well-structured and easy to follow. However, there are areas where you can improve, particularly in vocabulary, sentence structure, and providing more specific examples to support your arguments."
The kind of feedback a student can read three times and still not know what to change first.
The architecture

Five specialised agents, one feedback document.

A sequential pipeline. The first three score independent dimensions; the fourth synthesises; the fifth moderates. The list of original → corrected edits from A3 is preserved verbatim at the end of the output instead of being absorbed into the synthesised prose.

Input · student essay + prompt Output · single feedback document
A1 Structure
Evaluates organisation, paragraph flow, transitions.
Operates on the whole essay. Returns a structured score plus prose notes on coherence and section balance.
A2 Content
Measures prompt adherence, thesis quality, argumentation depth.
Reads the assignment prompt and the essay together; scores how well the writer addresses the task.
A3 Language
Scores grammar, vocabulary, register — and emits original → corrected error pairs.
The differentiator. Produces a structured list of edits with error types that survive into the final output instead of being absorbed into prose.
A4 Synthesizer
Merges the three evaluations into a coherent student-facing report.
Reads JSON outputs from A1/A2/A3 and produces a single human-readable feedback document.
A5 Moderator
Filters tone, bias, vagueness; produces the final text.
Final pass before delivery to the student. Strips evaluator artefacts, hedges, and any markers that could betray the system.
A3 — secret weapon
Specific corrections survive into the output.
A3 emits a structured array of original → corrected pairs with error types. A4 preserves the list verbatim at the end of the report instead of melting it into prose — so the student gets concrete edits, not generic advice.
  • Back in my senior high school time → During my senior high school years word_choice
  • causing my failure of every quiz → causing me to fail every quiz grammar
  • attending to cram schools → attending cram schools grammar
How we tested it

A three-condition controlled study, blind to the respondent.

We ran the same eight L2 English essays through three feedback sources, then shuffled the outputs into blind labels for survey participants.
